The Will

CAST: #1, #2

SCENE OPENS: We are in #1's office. #1 is a lawyer. He/she is preparing for a meeting. There is a knock at the door.

#1: Come on in. (#2 enters.) I'm glad you could make it. Sit down.

#2: I got your message. It sounded kind of urgent.

#1: It is.

#2: What's up?

#1: It has to do with the reading of your father's will tomorrow.

#2: And?

#1: Well, I thought we ought to talk about his will before then.

#2: What's to talk about? I know what's in it.

#1: When's the last time you two ... discussed it?

#2: I don't know. Must have been four ... or ... five ... (A horrible thought starts to dawn on #2) years ... no, wait. Do not tell me that he changed his will.

#1: See, this is why I wanted to meet now. I didn't want there to be a scene tomorrow.

#2: Why would there be a scene? The only reason I can think of is that he changed his will and I got screwed. And if that's the case ... fix it.

#1: Look, I want you to stay calm. I'm talking to you in the capacity as your lawyer ...

#2: Don't! Just show me the paper. (#1 takes out a document and gives it to #2. #2 looks it over and is shocked.)

#1: See, it's not really that bad.

#2: Not that bad? Not that bad?! I got fifty thousand dollars.

#1: That's a lot of money.

#2: When my father died he was worth forty million. My mother's dead, I have no brothers or sisters, my father has no other blood relatives ... so what happened to the other thirty-nine million, nine hundred and fifty thousand dollars?

#1: That's ... a little hard to tell you.

#2: I don't care, but let me warn you ... if you say he gave it to Shelley I'm going to reach down your throat and pull out your larynx. Now, where—did—the—money—go?!

#1: (Pause) He gave it to Shelley. (#1 lunges across the desk as #2 jumps out of the way.)

#2: I'm gonna kill you!!

#1: Why me? I'm just the lawyer.

#2: That's reason enough right there. But that aside, my father's already dead and someone must die for this.

#1: Just get a grip on yourself and sit down! (#1 and 2 both sit back down.)

#2: Why didn't you tell me this before?

#1: Because your father told me not to. I was bound by privilege. He said that if Shelley wanted to give you more that was up to her.

#2: Yeah, right. I've got a good picture of that. Well, there's got to be something we can do.

#1: Fine. Tell me what. Everything was legal.

#2: Contest the will!

#1: Just like that? On what grounds?

#2: I don't know! Think of something.

#1: There's nothing. He said he really loved her.

#2: Oh, please. Since my mother died that man hit on everything with a pulse between the ages of twenty and thirty. Now he meets a woman three months ago, marries her two months ago and drops forty million in her lap, and you find nothing wrong with that?

#1: Thirty-nine million, nine hundred and fifty thousand dollars, and it doesn't matter what I think. The courts won't find anything wrong with it. They need something illegal. They're kinda funny that way. (#2 thinks for a second.)

#2: OK ... how about if we say she coerced him?

#1: And you can prove this, of course. Proof is another thing they're fussy about.

#2: Why else would a twenty-four-year-old marry a seventy-year-old? Unless she was planning to take his money.

#1: So what?! Unless she did something illegal, they won't care why she married him.

#2: OK, OK ... how's this? We tell the courts he was crazy.

#1: Here we go with that old proof thing again. What do you have?

#2: Do you know how they found him?

#1: Not really.

#2: They found him dead on the floor dressed in a Cub Scout uniform.

#1: OK, but that really —

#2: Wait, it gets better. When questioned, Shelley said they were in the middle of playing his favourite game, "Den mother and the naughty scout." He was going for his merit badge in spankings when he keeled over. What do you think of that?

#1: I didn't know they gave out a merit badge for that. Still, a person's sexual proclivities are not a barometer of their mental capacities.

#2: (Pause) What the hell did you just say?

#1: Just because your father was weird, doesn't make him crazy.

#2: This is a nightmare.

#1: When he met her I told you to make friends with her. I warned you this might happen.

#2: You're a lawyer. Who listens to you?

#1: Well, you better listen now. He legally married her and legally changed his will. If you go into court, it's going to look like the greedy offspring versus the grieving widow.

#2: "Grieving widow," my butt. She knew exactly what she was doing.

#1: Maybe, but she was his wife, and technically, your stepmother.

#2: My stepmother? Then what does that make me? Cinderella? So now the evil stepmother dances off to the castle with forty million bucks and I get locked in the basement?

#1: Pretty much, unless you can find yourself one good fairy godmother.

#2: (Pause) No, wait! I've got something better. Remember that slimy little weasel we hired to investigate that last woman he wanted to marry?

#1: Was that the guy who couldn't come out in the daylight?

#2: Yeah. Call him. Get him to crawl up her ... past with a microscope and find me something that I can bargain with.

#1: You mean blackmail with.

#2: Bargain, blackmail, what's the difference? I need something!

#1: You know, this could get really ugly.

#2: No, it's already ugly. What it'll get is lethal if that bimbo walks away with my money.

#1: I've got to tell you, I'm against this. I think you should talk to her and try and work something out.

#2: You mean, be honest with her?

#1: Yes. I know it's a novel approach, but let's be different.

#2: Only as a last resort. For right now, call the weasel. Oh, and see if you can stall the reading of the will until we can get something on her.

#1: I'll do my best. (#2 gets up and starts to exit.)

#2: Call me later and let me know what's up.

#1: Where will you be?

#2: My house.

#1: That's the place your father owned in the city, right?

#2: Yeah.

#1: Maybe you should sit down again. I knew there was something I forgot to tell you ...

The End
